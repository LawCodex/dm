PRELIMINARY

### 1.

(1) This Act may be cited as the –
MEDICAL PROFESSION ACT, 2026.

Short title and
commencement.

(2) This Act shall come into force on such date as the
Minister may appoint by Order published in the Gazette.
Interpretation.

### 2.

(1) In this Act –
“Board” means the Medical Board established under section 3 of
the former Act;
“certificate of registration” means a certificate of registration
issued under section 22;
“Code of Ethics” means a Code of Ethics prescribed under
section 4(1)(i);
“Complaints Committee” means the Complaints Committee
referred to in section 6(1)(b);
“continuing medical education” means education or training that
the Council requires medical professionals to undertake by
regulations made under section 4(5);
“Council” means the Dominica Medical Council established
under section 3;
“dental practitioner” means a person who is qualified in the
practice of dentistry and whose name appears in the
register;
“Education and Training Committee” means the Education and
Training Committee referred to in section 6(1)(a);
“former Act” means the Medical Act;
“licence” means a valid licence to practise medicine issued under
section 34;

“licensed medical professional” means a medical professional
who holds a valid licence;
“Medical Association” means the Dominica Medical Association;
“medical professional” means a medical practitioner, dental
practitioner, specialist, osteopathic physician, doctorate
optometrist;
“medical practitioner” means a person other than a dental
practitioner who is qualified in the practice of medicine and
whose name appears in the register;
“medical registration authority” means a body established under
the law of another State or territory which has jurisdiction
similar to the functions of the Council under this Act;
“medicine” includes dentistry, surgery and optometry;
“Minister” means the Minister responsible for Health;
“negligence” includes failure on the part of a registered person to
exercise the proper and timely care expected from a
medical professional;
“professional misconduct” means conduct prohibited under section
47;
“register” means the Medical Register kept and maintained under
section 26 and includes a part of that register;
“Registrar” means the Registrar appointed under section 5;
“specialist” means a medical professional who has special training,
experience and qualifications, acceptable to the Council in
the areas specified by the Council and whose specialty is
entered in the register;

(2) A reference in any enactment to the Medical Board
shall be construed as a reference to the Council.

## PART I

DOMINICA MEDICAL COUNCIL
Establishment of
Dominica Medical
Council.

### 3.

(1) There is established a Council to be known as the
Dominica Medical Council.

Chap 3 :01.

(2) The Council shall be a body corporate to which section
55 of the Interpretation and General Clauses Act applies.

First Schedule.

(3) The First Schedule applies in respect to the constitution
of the Council and the other matters specified in the schedule.

Functions of the
Council.

### 4.

(1) The Council shall (a) register persons who satisfy the requirements of
this Act as to registration;
(b) issue licences to medical professionals who comply
with the requirements of this Act as to licensing;
(c) establish standards of proficiency necessary to
be registered;
(d) establish the standards of education and training
necessary to achieve the standards of proficiency
it has established under this section;
(e) regulate the standards of medical and dental
practice in the public interest;
(f) make recommendations for Regulations
prescribing the qualifications necessary in respect
of medical professionals;

(g) make recommendations for Regulations
prescribing requirements for the continuing
medical education of medical professionals as it
considers appropriate;
(i) make recommendations for Regulations
prescribing a Code of Ethics for the guidance of
medical professionals and standards relating to
the practice of medicine and professional
performance and may direct that a breach of the
Code of Ethics constitutes professional
misconduct;
(j) protect the public through the regulation of medical
professionals;
(k) monitor adherence to and investigate breaches of
this Act and a Code of Ethics made under this
Act;
(l) conduct investigations into professional conduct,
professional performance or the ability of medical
professionals to practise medicine;
(m) cause disciplinary proceedings to be taken in
accordance with this Act;
(n) issue certificates of good standing in the prescribed
form in respect of medical professionals when it
considers it appropriate;
(o) advise the Minister on any matter relating to its
functions and when requested by the Minister,
provide the Minister with information that he may
reasonably require;

(p) advise the Minister with respect to amendments
to the law relating to medicine as it considers
necessary;
(q) perform any other functions conferred on it by
this Act; and
(r) have the power to do all things necessary to
enable it to perform its functions.
(2) Without limiting the power conferred under subsection
(1)(r), the Council may –
(a) share information with other authorities
responsible for the registration of medical
professionals outside Dominica;
(b) develop or adopt training programmes in the
practice of medicine;
(c) cooperate with any university, hospital or
institution, whether in Dominica or elsewhere, in
order to provide for the education and evaluation
of medical professionals;
(d) participate in any programme relating to the
education of medical professionals or the practice
of medicine; and
(e) participate in the formulation of, and be a member
of, any body or programme concerned with
medical professionals.
(3) The Council shall consult with the Dominica Medical
Association and the Dominica Dental Association and any other
body of medical professionals as is appropriate before formulating
any codes and standards referred to in subsection (1).

(4) The Council shall consult the Education and Training
Committee before establishing standards under subsection (1)(c)
and (d) and qualifications under subsection (1)(f).
(5) The Council shall make recommendations respecting
Regulations to be made by the Minister requiring medical
professionals to undertake continuing medical education as is
specified in standards and recommendations and Regulations
made under this section may –
(a) provide for medical professionals who fail to
comply with any requirement of the regulations,
including provision for their registration or licence
to cease to have effect;
(b) require persons who have not practised or who
have not practised for or during a prescribed
period, to undertake education or training or to
gain experience as it specifies in standards.
(6) When the Council makes recommendations under
subsection (5), it shall include recommendations for establishing
the standards to be met in relation to –
(a) continuing medical education; or
(b) the education or training mentioned in subsection
(5)(b).
(7) The Council shall consult the Minister and have
regard to the advice of the Minister in establishing and executing
the policy of the Council.
(8) In the performance of its functions and the exercise of
its powers under this Act, the Council shall act impartially and in
the public interest.

## PART II

REGISTRATION AND LICENCE
Division 1 – Registration
General Registration.

### 17.

A person qualifies for general registration as a medical
professional where he –
(a) holds a medical degree or other equivalent form
of qualification granted by an accredited
University or School of Medicine that in the
opinion of the Council is evidence of satisfactory
training; or
(b) has successfully completed the examinations
administered by the Caribbean Association of
Medical Councils; and
(c) satisfies the Council that he –
(i) is a fit and proper person to be registered;
(ii) has successfully completed the prescribed
period of internship or an internship for the
practice of medicine that is substantially
equivalent to the prescribed internship; and
(iii) can read, write, speak and understand the
English language;

(iv) there is no other issue which the Council
considers would affect his ability to practise
medicine in Dominica competently and safely.

### 18.

(1) The Council may register as a specialist a person who
satisfies the Council that he qualifies for registration under section
17 and he has completed the prescribed tuition and training in a
speciality and has obtained the prescribed qualification from an
accredited institution or body recognised by the Council.
(2) For the purposes of this Act the Council recognises as
specialities the areas of medical and dental practice set out in the
Fifth Schedule;

Specialist registration.

Fifth Schedule.

### 19.

The Council may register, provisionally, a person who has
satisfied all of the qualification requirements specified in section
17, except that set out in section 17(c)(ii).

Provisional registration.

### 20.

The Council may temporarily register a person who
satisfies the Council that he is qualified to practise in any country
or place and he is -

Temporary registration.

(a) doing special work in the field of medicine or
dentistry and is sponsored by the University of the
West Indies or such other inter-governmental
organisation or body as the Minister approves
after consultation with the Council;
(b) a medical professional however styled, who is
employed, on a full time basis by an international
organisation that has an office in Dominica to
render medical or dental services exclusively and
without fee to members of that organisation;

(c) employed on a full time basis in the public service
and is working under the supervision of a consultant
or specialist medical practitioner.
Application for
registration.

### 21.

(1) A person who wishes to be registered as a medical
professional in Dominica shall (a) apply to the Council in the prescribed form;
(b) pay the prescribed application for registration
fee; and
(c) meet the requirements of this Act.
(2) An application under subsection (1) must be
accompanied by (a) evidence of the qualifications of the applicant;
(b) proof of identity of the applicant;
(c) proof that the applicant is a fit and proper person
to practise medicine in Dominica;
(d) a certificate of good standing from the applicant’s
previous registering body, where applicable; and
(e) any other documents or information that the
Council reasonably requires to decide the
application.
(3) A certificate of good standing under subsection (2)(d)
must include information as to –

(a) the professional conduct of the medical
professional;
(b) any complaints made by members of the public
against the medical professional and lodged with
the Council.
(4) Where the Council requires further information from
an applicant in order to make a decision respecting registration,
the Council shall promptly, in writing, request the information
required to make the decision.
(5) The Council shall make a determination respecting
the applicant’s qualification for registration and notify the applicant,
in writing, of that determination –
(a) within sixty days of the submission to the Council
by the applicant of the requested information; or
(b) immediately after the expiration of ninety days
from the date when the request was made, in the
case where the information requested by the
Council has not been received by the Council.

### 22.

(1) Where an application is made to the Council under
section 19 and the Council is satisfied that the applicant meets the
relevant requirements specified in section 17 and section 18 or 19
the Council shall on payment by the applicant of the prescribed
registration fee, register the applicant and subject to subsection
(2), issue a certificate of registration in the prescribed form in
respect of the applicant.
(2) A certificate of registration shall specify (a) the name and address of the holder of the certificate
of registration;
(b) the registration number of the registered person;

Issue of certificate of
registration.

(c) the category of registration;
(d) the nature and character of medical or dental
practice which may be performed.
(3) A certificate of registration is not transferable.
Refusal of registration.

### 23.

(1) The Council may refuse to register an applicant on the
following grounds:
(a) the applicant does not qualify for registration
under sections 17 to 19;
(b) the character of the applicant is such that it would
not be in the public interest to allow the applicant
to practise as a medical professional;
(c) the applicant has a physical or mental condition
which significantly impairs his ability to practise
as a medical professional;
(d) the applicant had previously been registered as a
medical professional in another jurisdiction and
his registration was cancelled on grounds that
would justify similar cancellation in Dominica.
(2) Where the Council, having considered an application
made under section 21 and any further information received, and
having regard to subsection (1) is of the opinion that the application
should be refused, the Council shall, subject to subsection (3),
notify the applicant in writing of its decision.
(3) In a notice under subsection (2), the Council shall give
reasons for the intention to refuse the application and shall inform
the applicant of his right to make written representations to the
Council under subsection (4).

(4) An applicant who receives a notice under subsection
(1) may make written representations to the Council within
twenty-one days after receipt of the notice.
(5) The Council shall have regard to any representations
made by the applicant within the time specified under subsection
(4) and shall, within fourteen days of the receipt of the written
representations, give notice in writing to the applicant of its
decision, the reasons for refusal, if any, and the applicant’s right
of appeal under section 58.

### 24.

(1) Where the Council refuses to register an applicant he
may within five months of the receipt by him of the notice or
refusal apply to the Council in writing for reconsideration of the
decision on the basis of submissions made by the applicant.

Application for
reconsideration of
decision.

(2) The Council may allow or deny the application for
reconsideration and where it allows reconsideration, may –
(a) refer the matter for review by a panel appointed
by the Council;
(b) confirm its earlier decision to refuse registration;
or
(c) register the applicant as a medical professional.

### 25.

The Council shall cancel the registration of a medical
professional where it is satisfied that the medical professional
does not qualify for registration or is disqualified from registration
by virtue of the circumstances referred to in section 23 (1)(b) to
(d).

Cancellation of
registration.

### 26.

(1) The Council shall keep and maintain a register to be
known as the Medical Register of every individual registered as
a medical professional in Dominica.

Register of medical
professionals.

(2) The Council shall cause the following information to
be entered in the register in subsection (1), in relation to every
person issued with a certificate of registration –
(a) his full name;
(b) date of birth;
(c) business address;
(d) the date of his registration;
(e) registration number;
(f) a description and date of the qualifications in
respect of which he is registered;
(g) area of specialty, where applicable;
(h) provisional status, where applicable;
(i) conditions of registration, if any; and
(j) any other information that the Council considers
appropriate.
(3) The Council may divide the Register in parts as
follows:
(a) Part 1 - General Registration;
(b) Part 2 – Specialist Registration;

### 46.

A medical professional shall –

(i) duty and responsibility of a medical professional

(ii) medical ethics.

### 47.

(1) A medical professional shall not –

(b) engage in behaviour that is contrary to medical

Prohibition of

DOMINICA MEDICAL COUNCIL
1\. (1) The Council shall consist of eight members appointed
by the Minister as follows:
(a) the Chairperson who shall be a person who has
been a registered medical practitioner in Dominica
for a continuous period of not less than ten years;

(b) three medical practitioners who have been
registered in Dominica for not less than five
years, on the nomination of the Dominica Medical
Association at least one of whom must be a
specialist;
(c) one dental practitioner who has been registered in
Dominica for not less than five years nominated
by the Dominica Medical Association;
(d) the Chief Medical Officer, who shall be an ex
officio member;
(e) an attorney-at-law nominated by the Dominica
Bar Association who has been admitted to practise
law in Dominica for not less than eight years;
(f) one member of the public who does not represent
any particular interest or body.
(2) The member appointed under subparagraph (1)(d) is
a non-voting member.
(3) The Council shall elect a Deputy Chairperson from the
members of the Council.
2\. (1) Subject to subparagraph (2), members of the Council,
other than the Chief Medical Officer shall hold office as follows:
(a) the Chairperson, for a term of three years;
(b) other members, for a term of two years.
(2) For the initial period of appointment to the Council, the
medical professionals referred to in paragraph 1(1)(b) and (c)
shall be appointed for three years.

(3) Members of the Council are eligible for re-appointment
and, except in respect of the Chief Medical Officer, shall hold
office for not more than two consecutive terms, but a member is
eligible for re-appointment after the expiration of one year
following the end of the two terms.
(4) The Chairperson of the Council shall continue to hold
office until a new Chairperson is appointed or until he is reappointed Chairperson, as the case may be.
3\. A person is disqualified from being a member of the
Council and shall not become or continue to be appointed as a
member of the Council if the person is or has been –

spent.

## THIRD SCHEDULE

(Sections 8(8) and 72)
PROCEEDINGS OF THE COMPLAINTS
COMMITTEE
1\. (1) On receiving a complaint in accordance with section
51 or on initiating an investigation of its own motion the Council
shall –
(a) record the date on which the complaint was
received or investigation initiated; and
(b) within thirty days of receipt or initiation of the
complaint or investigation, determine whether to
accept or reject the complaint.
(2) The Council may require the complainant to provide
further particulars of a complaint.
2\. Whenever a matter is referred by the Council to the
Complaints Committee, the Chairperson of the Committee shall
promptly notify, in writing, the medical professional whose conduct
or action is the subject of the investigation.
3\. The notice referred to in paragraph 2 shall –
(a) identify the matter that is to be investigated;
(b) shall stipulate the right of the medical professional
to submit to the Complaints Committee in writing,
any explanation or representation he wishes to
make as well as any document or other
information, concerning the matter that he wishes
to submit to the Committee;

(c) shall indicate that any submission must be made
by a date not later than twenty-eight days from
the date of the notice or such other time as
stipulated by the Complaints Committee.
4\. The Complaints Committee shall examine all documents
and other information relating to the complaint that are submitted
to it and shall make every reasonable effort to verify the information
and ascertain all the facts that are relevant to a determination of
the matter.
5\. The Complaints Committee may hear evidence from any
person, whether or not the person is a party concerned in the
matter.
6\. Following a consideration of the information submitted to
it, the Committee, where it determines that it is in the best interest
of all the parties concerned, may solicit clarifications, in writing,
with respect to any statement, representation or other information
submitted to the Committee.
7\. In the case where the Complaints Committee is conducting
an investigation other than an investigation under section 8(6)(a)
or (b) (a) the Committee may invite each of the parties or
both parties concerned in the matter to appear
before the Committee to make representations or
to answer questions respecting the matter;
(b) the Committee may, on the request of a party,
permit the party to appear before the Committee
and is not required to give notice to the other party
regarding the appearance but may allow the other
party to have a copy of any statement or
representation made to the Committee or an
extract of such statement or representation;

(c) the medical professional, where he appears before
the Committee, has the right to be represented by
an attorney-at-law.
8\. The Complaints Committee shall report, in writing, its
findings and decision and the reasons for its decision to the Council
in accordance with section 54.

## FOURTH SCHEDULE

(Sections 9(6) and 72)
PROCEEDINGS OF THE DICSCIPLINARY
COMMITTEE
1\. (1) The Council shall serve on the medical professional
against whom disciplinary proceedings are to be instituted a
notice of hearing which shall –
(a) specify in the form of a charge, the matters into
which the inquiry is to be held; and
(b) state the time and place at which the Disciplinary
Committee intends to hear the matter.
(2) Except with the consent of the medical professional,
a hearing shall not be fixed for a date earlier than twenty-eight
days after the date of the notice of hearing.
(3) The notice of a hearing shall be served personally or
by registered post on the medical professional at the address
shown on the register or at his last known address if that address
differs from that on the register.
2\. Where, after a complaint has been referred to the
Disciplinary Committee for investigation, it appears
to the
Disciplinary Committee that the hearing should not be held, the

Council shall notify the complainant, if any, and the medical
professional of the fact.
3\. The medical professional is entitled to receive free copies
of, or to be allowed access to, any documentary evidence relied
on for the purpose of the hearing.
4\. The medical professional has the right to be represented
by an attorney-at-law in any disciplinary proceedings instituted
against him.
5\. Where the medical professional does not appear at the
date fixed for the hearing of the case, the Disciplinary Committee
may, if it is satisfied that a notice of hearing has been served on
the medical professional, proceed with the hearing in his absence
and the absence of his attorney may proceed with the hearing, and
without further notice to the medical professional, take any action
that it is authorised to take under this Act.
6\. (1) Where witnesses are examined by the Disciplinary
Committee, the medical professional shall be given an opportunity
of attending and of putting questions to the witnesses in his own
behalf; and no documentary evidence shall be used against him
unless he has previously been supplied with copies of those
documents or given access to the documents.
(2) The medical professional shall be permitted to give
evidence, call witnesses and make submissions orally or in writing
in his own behalf.
(3) The Disciplinary Committee may call additional
witnesses and may adjourn the proceedings to another convenient
time or place.
(4) The testimony of witnesses shall be taken under oath,
which the Chairperson of the Disciplinary Committee is authorised
to administer, and there shall be a full right to examine, cross-

examine and re-examine witnesses.
(5) The Disciplinary Committee may issue summons for
the purposes of procuring the attendance of witnesses, the giving
of testimony and the production of documents before the
Disciplinary Committee.
(6) The rules of evidence in a hearing, and the proceedings
and penalties in the case of disobedience to any summon, shall be
the same as in civil cases in the Supreme Court.
7\. The Disciplinary Committee shall have a record of the
proceedings and the evidence (a) taken in shorthand, transcribed and certified by a
professional transcriber; or
(b) recorded by a sound recording machine, and
transcribed and certified by a professional
transcriber.
8\. Where, having heard the evidence in support of the
charges, the Disciplinary Committee is of the opinion that the
evidence is insufficient, it may dismiss the charges without calling
on the medical professional for his defence.
9\. On the conclusion of the hearing, the Disciplinary
Committee shall submit a report to the Council in accordance with
section 54.
