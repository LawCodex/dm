AN ACT TO INSTITUTE A SYSTEM TO FACILITATE
THE PROVISION OF ADVANCE INFORMATION
RELATING TO PASSENGERS AND CREW MEMBERS OF AN AIRCRAFT OR A VESSEL; TO
ESTABLISH PROTOCOLS FOR THE SHARING
OR SUCH INFORMATION WITH OTHER
STATES FOR THE PURPOSE OF IDENTIFYING
PERSONS WHO MAY POSE RISKS TO SECURITY AND FOR CONNECTED MATTERS.
(Gazetted 5th July, 2018.)
BE IT ENACTED by the Parliament of the Commonwealth of
Dominica as follows:

### 1.

(1) This Act may be cited as the –
ADVANCE PASSENGER
INFORMATION SYSTEM ACT, 2018.
(2) This Act shall come into force on such date as the
Minister may appoint by Order published in the Gazette.

Short title and
commencement.

Interpretation.

### 2.

In this Act –
“advance passenger information” means the information or data
concerning a crew member, passenger or any other person
travelling in an aircraft or a vessel as set out in the First
Schedule.
“agent” means a person who is authorised in writing by the owner,
captain or master of an aircraft or a vessel to perform any
function under this Act on behalf of the owner, captain or
master;
“APIS” means the system of automated electronic data interchange of advance passenger information developed by
the Caribbean Community for the benefit of Member
States of the Community and the screening of advance
passenger information by IMPACS and JRCC against a
watch list for an API hit;
“aircraft” includes an aeroplane, helicopter, or other means of
airborne navigation by means of which persons can travel
across international borders;
“API hit” means a name present in one or more watch lists;
“captain” means the pilot of an aircraft designated by the operator,
or in the case of general aviation, the owner or pilot
designated by the owner, as being in command and charged
with the safe conduct of the flights;
“CARICOM Council for National Security and Law Enforcement” has the meaning assigned in the Treaty;
“commercial aircraft” means an aircraft which engages in transporting passengers or goods for monetary gain;
“commercial vessel” means a vessel which engages in transporting passengers or goods for monetary gain;

“competent authority” means the office specified in or office or
entity designated under section 4;
“crew member” means (a) in relation to an aircraft, a licensed individual charged
with duties that are essential to the operation of the
aircraft during a flight;
(b) in relation to a vessel, an individual charged with
performing duties that are essential to the operation of
a vessel and the safety, entertainment and well-being
of passengers during a journey;
“IMPACS” means the Implementing Agency for Crime and
Security established under the 2006 Agreement establishing the CARICOM Implementing Agency for Crime and
Security;
“INTERPOL” means the International Criminal Police Organisation;
“JRCC” means the Joint Regional Communications Centre established as a sub-agency of IMPACS and utilized for prescreening of travellers to, or from, a Member State of the
Caribbean Community;
“master” includes every person having command or charge of a
vessel other than a pilot;
“Minister” means the Minister responsible for Immigration;
“Participating Countries” means Member States of the Treaty;
“passenger” means any person, not being a bona fide member of
the crew, travelling or seeking to travel on an aircraft or
a vessel;

“private aircraft” means any aircraft which is not a commercial
or state aircraft;
“technical stop” means the arrival of an aircraft or a vessel in
Dominica for purposes of refueling, repairs, or as a result
of an accident, unfavourable weather conditions, emergency, or other similar purpose, but does not include the
taking on or discharging of passengers, baggage, cargo or
mail;
“Treaty” means the Revised Treaty of Chaguaramas establishing
the Caribbean Community including the CARICOM Single Market and Economy signed at Nassau, The Bahamas,
on 5th July 2001;
“vessel” means a ship, boat, yacht or other floating or submersible
transportation, including amphibious vehicles, hydrofoil
craft amd hovercraft, by means of which persons can
travel across international borders;
“watch list” means a list that is used to watch or track the activities
or movements of –
(a) criminals including–
(i) terrorists or suspected terrorists and persons who
have been found guilty of involvement with stolen
and lost travel documents;
(ii) criminal deportees; and
(b) other persons of interest to the intelligence community.
Application.

### 3.

This Act applies to any aircraft or vessel which is
expected to arrive in Dominica or to leave Dominica.

### 4.

(1) Subject to subsection (2), the Competent Authority is
the Chief Immigration Officer.

Competent Authority

(2) The Minister may by Order designate another officer
or entity as the Competent Authority.
(3) For purposes of subsection (1) “Chief Immigration
Officer” includes Commissioner of Police and any police officer
or any constable or other person duly authorised by the Commissioner of Police to execute the provisions of this Act.

### 5.

(1) Subject to subsection (4) and section 7, the captain,
master or agent of every aircraft or vessel which is expected to
arrive in Dominica or leave Dominica shall provide the advance
passenger information to the competent authority and IMPACS
within the time frames set out in the Second Schedule.
(2) A captain, master or agent of an aircraft or a vessel
who –
(a) fails to provide the advance passenger information in accordance with subsection (1);
(b) intentionally or recklessly provides erroneous,
faulty, misleading, incomplete or false advance
passenger information; or
(c) transmits the advance passenger information in
an incorrect format,
is guilty of an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine
of ten thousand dollars or imprisonment for one year.
(3) It shall be a defence for a captain, master or agent of
an aircraft or a vessel to satisfy the competent authority that it was
not practicable to provide the advance passenger information
within the time frame set out in the Second Schedule, or where the
advance passenger information is inaccurate, that the error was

such circumstances and subject conditions as the Minister may
specify in such waiver where the aircraft or vessel is –
(a) a military or law enforcement aircraft or vessel; or
(b) on official state business.
Verification of advance
passenger information.

### 6.

(1) The competent authority shall verify the advance
passenger information provided under section 5 during physical
processing at any port by using the travel documents provided by
crew members or passengers.
(2) Where a discrepancy is found in the advance passenger information, the competent authority shall –
(a) correct the information or data at the port of entry
or exit; and
(b) update the information or data in the APIS,
after verifying it from the travel or other relevant documents of the
crew member or passenger.
(3) The captain, master or agent, and a crew member or
passenger, of an aircraft or a vessel shall –
(a) answer any question asked by the competent
authority relating to the aircraft or vessel and its
crew members, passengers, voyage or flight and
any persons that are carried by the aircraft or
vessel; and

(b) forthwith at the request of the competent authority, produce any documents within that person’s
possession or control relating to the aircraft or
vessel and its crew members, passengers, voyage or flight or other persons that are carried by
the aircraft or vessel.
(4) A person referred to in subsection (3) who –
(a) refuses to answer any question asked under
subsection (3)(a), or knowingly gives a false
answer, to a question put to such person by the
competent authority under subsection (3)(a); or
(b) fails to comply with a request to produce documents made under subsection (3)(b),
is guilty of an offence and is liable on summary conviction to fine
of five thousand dollars or to imprisonment for six months.

### 7.

(1) Section 5 does not apply to an aircraft or a vessel which
lands, berths, anchors, or otherwise arrives in Dominica if the
arrival is –
(a) required by any statutory or other requirement
relating to navigation;
(b) a technical stop; or
(c) authorised by the competent authority.
(2) Where an aircraft or a vessel arrives or stops for a
reason referred to in subsection (1), the captain, master or
agent –
(a) shall forthwith report to the competent authority;

Technical or non-traffic
stops.

(b) shall not, except with the consent and authorization of the competent authority, permit a crew
member or passenger to disembark from the
aircraft or vessel; and
(c) shall comply with any directions given by the
competent authority in respect of any crew members or passengers carried on the aircraft or
vessel.
(3) A crew member or passenger of an aircraft or a vessel
shall not, without the consent and authorisation of the competent
authority, disembark from the aircraft or vessel and all such
persons shall comply with any directions given by the competent
authority.
(4) A captain, master or agent who fails to comply with
subsection (2) or (3) is guilty of an offence and is liable on
summary conviction to a fine of ten thousand dollars or to
imprisonment for one year.
(5) Notwithstanding any provision of this section, the
disembarkation of crew members or passengers from an aircraft
or a vessel shall not constitute an offence where the disembarkation is necessary for reasons of health, safety or the preservation
of life.
Power and duties of
competent authority.

### 8.

(1) The competent authority shall –
(a) co-ordinate with the IMPACS on all matters
relating to the APIS;
(b) if requested and subject to subsection (2), allow a
crew member or passenger from an aircraft or a
vessel to access their personal identifiable information maintained in the APIS to ensure its
correctness;

(c) determine, after consultation with IMPACS, the
admissibility or otherwise of passengers or crew
members;
(d) withhold clearance for the departure of an aircraft or a vessel whose captain, master or agent
has not provided the advance passenger information required under section 5, pending submission
of the advance passenger information;
(e) assess the sufficiency, and error rates, in review
of advance passenger information transmissions
for each journey;
(f) take any other action or measure that he considers appropriate to ensure that accurate and relevant advance passenger information is provided
in accordance with this Act.
(2) A crew member or passenger of an aircraft or a vessel
shall not have access to any information provided by IMPACS or
JRCC through the screening of advance passenger information
by IMPACS and JRCC against a watch list for any API hit.
(3) Notwithstanding subsection (1)(a) and (c) any decision with respect to the admissibility or inadmissibility of a crew
member or passenger from an aircraft or a vessel shall be made
solely by the competent authority.

### 9.

(1) Advance passenger information shall be used solely
for the purposes of the Act.
(2) IMPACS shall use the APIS to conduct screening
against watch lists of crew members and passengers on aircraft
and vessels that enter into, depart from, and travel within, the
regional space in order to provide information to assist Participating Countries.

Use and sharing of
information and the
APIS.

(3) IMPACS may share the information contained within
the APIS, for the purpose of furthering national, regional or
international security, with –
(a) INTERPOL; and
(b) any other national, regional or international intelligence, law enforcement, or security agencies
approved by CARICOM Council for National
Security and Law Enforcement.
Retention of advance
passenger information.

### 10.

(1) Advance passenger information collected under this
Act for entry screening purposes shall be retained for a period not
exceeding three years from the date of travel of the crew member
or passenger of the aircraft or vessel.
(2) Subsection (1) does not apply to information or data
copied from the APIS into any other security database system to
which a different data retention schedule applies.

Regulations.

### 11.

(1) The Minister may make regulations to give effect to
the principles and provisions of this Act.
(2) Without prejudice to the generality of subsection (1),
the Minister may make regulations prescribing –
(a) procedures for collaboration and coordination of
information gathering and sharing under this Act;
and
(b) offences for contraventions of any of the provisions of regulations made under this Act and the
penalties shall not exceed ten thousand dollars for
any offence prescribed.

Amendment of
Schedules.

### 12.

The Minister may by Order published in the Gazette, after
consultation with CARICOM Council for National Security and
Law Enforcement, amend the First Schedule or Second Schedule.

## SECOND SCHEDULE

TIME FRAMES FOR PROVISION OF ADVANCE
PASSENGER INFORMATION
1\.

In case of a commercial aircraft or vessel, arriving from a
destination within the regional space, no later than 40 minutes
prior to its departure from the last port of call;

2\.

In case of a private aircraft or vessel, arriving from a d e s tination within the regional space, no later than 40 minutes
prior to its departure from the last port of call;

3\.

In case of an aircraft or a vessel arriving from a destination
outside the regional space, no later than 24 hours prior to
arrival of that aircraft or vessel;

4\.

In the case of a commercial aircraft or vessel an API file is
required to be submitted to the competent authority no later
than five (5) minutes immediately following the departure of
that aircraft or vessel.

5\.

In the case of a private aircraft or vessel an API file is
required to be submitted to the competent authority prior to
departure of that aircraft or vessel.

6\.

In the event of any changes to the aircraft or vessel header
data or data relating to an individual on board, an updated

API file is required to be submitted to the competent authority
prior to departure of that aircraft or vessel.
