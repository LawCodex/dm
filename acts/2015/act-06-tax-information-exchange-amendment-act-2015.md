# Tax Information Exchange (Amendment) Act 2015

COMMONWEALTH OF DOMINICA
ACT NO. 6 OF 2015
I assent

LS

CHARLES A. SAVARIN
President
30, July 2015
AN ACT TO AMEND THE TAX INFORMATION EXCHANGE ACT, CHAPTER 67:02.
(Gazetted 30th July , 2015)
BE IT ENACTED by the Parliament of the Commonwealth of
Dominica as follows:

### 1.

This Act may be cited as the –
TAX INFORMATION EXCHANGE
(AMENDMENT) ACT, 2015.

Short title.

Interpretation.
Chap. 67:02.

### 2.

In this Act the Tax Information Exchange Act is
referred to as “the Act”.

Repeal and replacement
of section 2 of the Act.

### 3.

Section 2 of the Act is repealed and replaced by the
following:
“Interpretation.

TAX INFORMATION
EXCHANGE (AMENDMENT)

ACT 6

2\. In this Act “agreement” means an agreement for the provision
of information in taxation matters, being an
agreement which has legal effect in Dominica
and which is more particularly set out in the
schedule;
“Comptroller” means the Comptroller of Inland
Revenue;
“electronic” means relating to technology having
electrical, magnetic, optical, electromagnetic,
or similar capabilities, whether digital, analogue or otherwise;
“electronic record” means a record processed and
maintained by electronic means;
“information” means any fact, statement, document
or record in whatever form; and includes (a)

any fact, statement, document or
record held by banks, other financial
institutions or any persons, including
nominees and trustees, acting in an
agency or fiduciary capacity; and

any fact, statement, document or
record regarding the beneficial ownership of companies, partnerships and

other persons, including (i)

in the case of collective investment funds, information on
shares, units and other interests; and

(ii)

in the case of trusts, information on settlors, trustees and
beneficiaries;

“items subject to legal privilege” means (a)

communications between an attorney-at-law and his client or any person representing his client made in
connection with the giving of legal
advice to the client;

(b)

communications between an attorney-at-law and his client or any person representing his client or between
such attorney-at-law or his client or
any such representative and any other
person made in connection with or in
contemplation of legal proceedings
and for the purposes of such proceedings; and

(c)

items enclosed with or referred to in
such communications and made (i)

in connection with the giving of
legal advice; or

(ii)

in connection with or in contemplation of legal proceedings

and for the purposes of such
proceedings, when they are in
the possession of a person who
is entitled to possession of them;
but items held with the intention of furthering a
criminal purpose are not subject to legal privilege;
“Minister” means the Minister responsible for Finance;
“Party” means a party to an agreement;
“proceedings” means civil or criminal proceedings;
“relevant tax laws” means the tax laws pertaining to
the taxes to which an agreement applies;
“request” means a request for information made by
one of the Parties to the other Party pursuant
to an agreement;
“requesting Party” means a Party making a request;
“the Act” means the Income Tax Act.”.
Amendment of section 4
of the Act.

### 4.

Section 4 of the Act is amended by deleting the words
“of Inland Revenue”.

Insertion of new sections
4A, 4B, 4C and 4D.

### 5.

The Act is amended by inserting the following new
sections 4A, 4B, 4C and 4D after section 4:
“Production
ofinformation.

4A.(1) Where, under a request, the Comptroller
considers it necessary to obtain specified information or information of a specified description from
any person the Comptroller shall (2) Where information is produced under
subsection (1)(b) -

(a)

in the case of information required for
proceedings in the territory of the
requesting Party or related investigations, apply to a Judge of the High
Court for an order to produce such
information; or

(b)

in the case other than that referred to
in paragraph (a), issue a notice in
writing requiring the production of
such information as may be specified
in the notice; and such notice may
require the information to be –
(i)

provided within a specified time;

(ii)

provided in such form as the
Comptroller may require; and

(iii) verified or authenticated in such
manner as the Comptroller may
require.
(3) Where information is produced under
subsection (1)(b) (a)

the Comptroller may take copies or
extracts of any information; and

(b)

where a person claims a lien on a
document, the production is without
prejudice to his lien.

(4) An order under subsection (5) or a notice
under subsection (1)(b) (a)

shall not confer any right to production

of, or access to, items subject to legal
privilege; and
(b)

shall have effect notwithstanding any
obligation as to confidentiality or other
restriction upon the disclosure of information whether imposed by law,
including the common law.

(5) If, on an application under subsection
(1)(a), the Judge is satisfied that the conditions in
subsection (7) are fulfilled, he may make an order
that the person who appears to him to be in possession or control of the information to which the
application relates shall (a)

produce it to the Comptroller or his
authorised representative to take away;
or

(b)

give the Comptroller or his authorised
representative access to it, within such
period as the order may specify.

(6) The period to be specified in an order
under subsection (5) shall be fourteen days, unless it
appears to the Judge that a longer or shorter period
would be appropriate in the particular circumstances
of the application.
(7) The conditions referred to in subsection
(5) are that the Judge is satisfied that (a)

the Comptroller or his authorised
representative has certified that the
request is in compliance with the
relevant agreement set out in the

schedule;

the information to which the request
relates is under the possession or control of a person in Dominica;

(c)

the information to which the request
relates does not include items subject
to legal privilege or items subject to
protection as secret, pursuant to the
terms of the relevant agreement or
this Act, as the case may be;

(d)

there has been compliance with section 5B; and

(e)

pursuant to the terms of the relevant
agreement there are no reasonable
grounds for not granting the request.

(8) Where the Judge makes an order under
subsection (5)(b) in relation to information held on
any premises he may, on the application of the
Comptroller, order any person who appears to him to
be entitled to grant entry to the premises to allow the
Comptroller or his authorised representative, with or
without a police officer, to enter the premises to
obtain access to the information.
(9) The Chief Justice may make rules governing the procedure in relation to (a)

applications for the grant, discharge
and variation of orders under subsection (5); and

proceedings relating to such orders.

(10) Where the information to which an
order under subsection (5) or a notice under subsection
(1)(b) relates consists of information maintained as
an electronic record, such information shall be produced in a form in which it can be taken away and in
which it is visible and legible or in a form in which it
is visible and legible, as the case may be.
(11) Where, pursuant to a request concerning criminal proceedings or investigations, an order
under subsection (5) has been made or has been
applied for, and has not been refused, or a warrant
under section 4C(2) has been issued, whoever, knowing or suspecting that a request has been made, or that
an investigation into any matter to which a request
relates is taking place, makes any disclosure which is
likely to prejudice the proceedings or the investigation
to which the request may relate, commits an offence
and is liable on summary conviction to a fine of ten
thousand dollars and to imprisonment for three years.
(12) In proceedings against a person for an
offence under subsection (11), it is a defence to prove (a)

that he did not know or believe that the
disclosure was likely to prejudice the
request or investigation; or

that he had lawful authority or reasonable excuse for making the disclosure.

(13) A person required to produce information under subsection (5) shall have the right to be
represented by an attorney-at-law when he does so.

Notification.

4B. (1) Subject to subsection (2), a person who
is the subject of a request for information solely in
relation to a matter which is not a criminal matter or
an alleged criminal matter, shall if his whereabouts or
address is made known to the Comptroller, be served
with a notice by the Comptroller advising of the
following (a)

the existence of a request specifying
that person;

(b)

the jurisdiction making the request;
and

(c)

the general nature of the information
sought,

and any person so notified may within fifteen days
from the date of receipt of the notice, make a written
submission to the Comptroller specifying any grounds
which he wishes the Comptroller to consider in
making his determination as to whether or not the
request is in compliance with the provisions of the
relevant agreement, including any assertions that the
information requested is subject to legal privilege.
(2) The Comptroller shall consider any written
submission made in compliance with subsection (1),
but shall not be obliged to permit or consider any oral
submission by or on behalf of any person who is the
subject of a request.
(3) Nothing in this Act requires the
Comptroller to search for or conduct enquiries into
the address or whereabouts of any person who is the
subject of a request in order to serve a notice to that
person pursuant to subsection (1).

(4) For the purposes of subsection (1), the
date of receipt of notice from the Comptroller is
deemed to be fifteen days from the date of issuance
indicated on the notice.
Enforcement

4C. (1) Where pursuant to a request, the
Comptroller considers it necessary to enter and
search any premises, the Comptroller shall apply to
the High Court for the issue of a search warrant for
specified premises to search for and seize specified
information or information of a specified description.
(2) On application made under subsection
(1), the High Court may issue a warrant authorising
the search and seizure if it is satisfied that (a)

an order made or notice issued under
section 4A in relation to information
on the premises has not been complied
with;

(b)

the request for the purposes of which
the application is made might be seriously prejudiced unless a police officer could secure immediate access
to the information; or

(c)

the conditions in section 4A(7) are
fulfilled in relation to any information
on the premises; and

(d)

it would not be appropriate to make an
order under section 4A(5) in relation
to the material because (i)

it is not practicable to communi-

cate with any person entitled to
produce the information;
(ii) it is not practicable to communicate with any person entitled to
grant access to the information
or entitled to grant entry to the
premises on which the information is situated; or
(iii) the request for the purposes of
which the application is made
might be seriously prejudiced
unless a police officer could
secure immediate access to the
information.
(3) Any information seized under a warrant
issued under subsection (2) shall be brought immediately to the Comptroller to be dealt with according to
law.
Protection of
persons
disclosing
confidential
information.

4D. A person who divulges any confidential
information in conformity with an order or notice
issued pursuant to a request is deemed not to commit
any offence under any law for the time being in force
in Dominica, by reason only of the disclosure; and
such disclosure is deemed not to be a breach of any
confidential relationship between that person and
any other person, and no civil claim or action whatsoever shall lie against the person making the disclosure or against the person’s principal or employer by
reason of the disclosure.”.
