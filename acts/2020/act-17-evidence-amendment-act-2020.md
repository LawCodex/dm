### 2.

In this Act the Evidence Ordinance is referred to as
“the Ordinance”.

### 3.

Section 7 of the Ordinance is amended by deleting the
words “the Schedule” and substituting the words “Schedule 1”.

### 4.

The Ordinance is amended by inserting the following new
section 12A immediately after section 12:
“Admissibility
of documentary
records.

12A. (1) The Court may allow the admission of
a statement in a document as evidence of any fact
stated therein of which direct viva voce evidence
would be admissible, if the court is satisfied that (a) the document is or forms part of a record
compiled by a person acting under a duty, from
information supplied by another person, whether
the other person was acting under a duty or not,
who had, or may be reasonably supposed to
have had personal knowledge of the matters
dealt with in that information;
(b) the person who supplied the information (i)

is dead or by reason of his bodily or mental
condition is unfit to attend as a witness;

(ii) is outside of Dominica and it is not reasonably practicable to secure his attendance;
(iii) cannot reasonably be expected, having
regard to the time that has elapsed since
he supplied the information and to all the
circumstances, to have any recollection of
the matters dealt with in that information;
(c) all reasonable steps have been taken to identify

the person who supplied the information but he
cannot be identified; or
(d) though the identity of the person who supplied
the information is known, all reasonable steps
have been taken to find him, but he cannot be
found.
(2) Subsection (1) applies (a) whether the information contained in the document was supplied directly;
(b) whether the information contained in the document was, subject to subsection (3), supplied
indirectly; or
(c) where the person compiling the record is himself the person by whom the information is
supplied.
(3) If the information contained in the document was supplied indirectly, subsection (1) only
applies if each person through whom the information
was supplied was acting under a duty.
(4) Where a document contains evidence
that a person could be expected to give if called as a
witness, and the document has been prepared for the
purpose of any pending or contemplated proceedings, a statement contained in the document shall not
be given in evidence without leave of the Court.
(5) The Court shall not give leave to admit a
statement referred to in subsection (4) unless the
Court is of the opinion that the statement ought to be
admitted in the interest of justice having regard to -

(a) the circumstances in which leave is sought and
in particular, to the contents of the statement;
and
(b) the likelihood that the defendant will be prejudiced by the admission of the statement in the
absence of the person who supplied the information on which the statement is based.
(6) If a statement that is based on information supplied by a person is given in evidence in a
proceeding (a) any evidence which, if the person had been
called as a witness, would have been admissible
as relevant to his credibility as a witness is
admissible for that purpose in those proceedings;
(b) evidence may, with the leave of the Court, be
given of any matter which if the person had
been called as a witness, would have been put
to him in cross-examination as relevant to his
credibility as a witness but of which evidence
could not have been adduced by the crossexamination of that party; and
(c) evidence tending to prove that the person,
whether before or after supplying the information, made a statement, whether oral or not, that
is inconsistent with the information, is admissible for the purpose of showing that the person
has contradicted himself.
(7) A statement which is admissible is not
capable of corroborating evidence given by the
person who supplied the information on which the
statement is based.

(8) In estimating the weight, if any, to be
attached to a statement admissible in evidence,
regard shall be had to all the circumstances from
which any inference can reasonably be drawn as to
the accuracy or otherwise of the statement and in
particular to the question whether or not the
person (a)

who supplied the information from which the
record containing the statement was compiled
did so contemporaneously with the occurrence
and existence of the facts dealt with in that
information; and

(b)

any other person concerned with compiling or
keeping the record containing the statement
had any incentive to conceal or misrepresent
the facts.

(9) The Court may act on a certificate
purporting to be signed by a registered medical
practitioner when deciding whether a person is unfit
to attend as a witness under subsection (1)(b)(i).
(10) Nothing in this section shall prejudice
the admissibility of evidence that would be admissible
apart from this section.
(11) In this section, a reference to a person
acting under a duty includes a reference to a person
acting in the course of any trade, business, profession
or other occupation in which he is engaged or
employed or for the purposes of any paid or unpaid
office held by him.”.

### 5.

Section 13 of the Ordinance is repealed and replaced
as follows:

Repeal and replacement
of section 13 of the
Ordinance.

“Admissibility of expert
reports.

ACT 17

13\. (1) An expert report is admissible in evidence
in any proceeding in any court as prima facie
evidence of any fact that is of consequence to the
determination of the proceeding.
(2) An expert report is not inadmissible solely
on the basis that any fact stated therein is (a) an ultimate issue to be determined in criminal
proceedings; or
(b) a matter of common knowledge.
(3) If an expert report is based on a fact that
is outside the general body of knowledge that makes
up the expertise of the expert, the Court may rely on
the report if the fact is or will be proved or judicially
noticed in the proceeding.

Schedule 2.

(4) The Court shall not admit in evidence an
expert report that is not in conformity with the content
requirements set out in Schedule 2.
(5) Where an expert witness is unable to give
viva voce evidence, the Court may allow the admission of an expert report in criminal proceedings as
evidence of any fact stated therein of which direct
viva voce evidence would be admissible, if the Court
is satisfied that the expert witness (a)

is dead or by reason of his bodily or mental
condition unfit to attend as a witness;

(b)

is outside of Dominica and it is not reasonably
practicable to secure his attendance; or

(c)

cannot reasonably be expected, having regard
to the time that has elapsed since he supplied or
acquired the information and to all the circumstances, to have any recollection of the matters
dealt with in that information.

(6) Where an expert report is admissible
under subsection (5), the judge or coroner shall direct
the jury to be cautious in evaluating the report having
regard to the following:
(a) the expert witness was not required to take an
oath prior to his preparation of the report; and
(b) the lack of opportunity of the party against
whom the expert report is being admitted, to
cross-examine the expert witness.
(7) Notwithstanding subsection (5), the
Court may call an expert witness to give viva voce
evidence (a) on application by a party in the proceedings or
at an inquest; or
(b) on its own motion.
(8) Where an expert witness is called to give
viva voce evidence on the subject matter of his
report, the party calling him shall pay all costs
occasioned, unless the Court orders otherwise.
(9) The Court may act on a certificate
purporting to be signed by a registered medical
practitioner when deciding whether an expert is unfit
to attend as a witness under subsection (5)(a).”.

### 6.

The Ordinance is amended by the insertion of the
following new section 33 immediately after section 32:
“Evidence by
live link.

33\. (1) The Court may issue a direction that
permits evidence to be given by means of a live link.
(2) The Court may give permission for the
purposes of subsection (1) if it appears to the Court
to be in the interests of justice to do so either on –
(a) an application by a party to a proceeding; or
(b) its own motion.
(3) In determining whether a direction to
permit evidence by live link is appropriate, the Court
shall take into consideration the following:
(a) the age of the witness;
(b) that the witness has a physical disability or
disorder and is unable to attend as a witness;
(c) all the circumstances of the case and in particular —
(i)

the availability of the witness;

(ii) the need for the witness to attend in
person;
(iii) the importance of the witness’ evidence to
the proceedings;
(iv) any views expressed by the witness;
(v) the suitability of the facilities at the place

where the witness would give evidence
through a live link;
(vi) whether a direction appears to inhibit any
party to the proceedings from effectively
testing the witness’ evidence; or
(d) the condition under section 13(5)(b).
(5) In a live link direction, the Court may
provide for a specified person to accompany the
witness while the witness gives evidence.
(6) Evidence given under subsection (1)
shall be given as though the witness was physically
before the Court and the law relating to contempt of
court with respect to a refusal by the witness to
answer a question or to produce a document, or to
perjury applies to such evidence.
(7) Where in a trial evidence has been
given in accordance with subsection (1), the judge
shall give the jury a warning, if the judge considers
this necessary to ensure that the fact that the
evidence was given by means of a live link, does not
prejudice the accused.
(8) The Court may, on an application by a
party to the proceedings or on its own motion,
discharge, vary or further vary a direction that
provides for a witness to give evidence by means of
a live link, if the Court is satisfied that it is in the
interests of justice to do so.
(9) In this section, “live link” means a technological arrangement whereby a witness, without

being physically present in the place where proceedings are held, is able to see and hear and be seen,
heard, examined and cross-examined.”.

### 8.

The Ordinance is amended by the insertion of the
following new Schedule 2 immediately after Schedule 1:
“SCHEDULE 2

(Section 13)

Content of an Expert Report
An expert report must (a) be accompanied by a curriculum vitae detailing the
expert’s qualifications, relevant experience and accreditation;
(b) set out the substance of all material instructions
including the basis on which the expert is asked to
advise;
(c) give details of any literature or other information
which the expert has relied on in making the report;
(d) contain a statement setting out the substance of all
facts given to the expert which are material to the
opinions expressed in the report, or upon which those
opinions are based;
(e) make clear which of the facts stated in the report are
within the expert’s own knowledge;

(f) contain particulars of any examination, measurement,
test or experiment which the expert has conducted on
which the report is based;
(g) say who carried out any examination, measurement,
test or experiment which the expert has used for the
report and (i) give the qualifications, relevant experience and
accreditation of that person,
(ii) say whether or not the examination, measurement, test or experiment was carried out under
the expert’s supervision, and
(iii) summarise the findings on which the expert relies;
(h) where there is a range of opinion on the matters dealt
with in the report (i) summarise the range of opinion, and
(ii) give reasons for the expert’s own opinion;
(i) if the expert is not able to give his opinion without
qualification, state the qualification;
(j) include such information as the Court may need to
decide whether the expert’s opinion is sufficiently
reliable to be admissible as evidence;
(k) contain a summary of the conclusions reached;
(l) contain a statement that the expert understands an
expert’s duty to the Court, and has complied and will
continue to comply with that duty;

(m) be verified by a statement of truth in the following
form of words:
“I confirm that in so far as the facts stated in my report
are within my own knowledge, I have made clear
which they are and I believe them to be true, and that
the opinions I have expressed represent my true and
complete professional opinion”; and
(n) contain the administrative information and protocol
used.”.
