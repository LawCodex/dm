## PART I

PRELIMINARY
1\. This Act may be cited as the MEDICAL SCHOOLS (ACCREDITATION)
ACT, 2008.

Short title.

Interpretation.

2\. In this Act –
“accredit” means the recognition by the Medical Board, on the
basis of an informed evaluation of the structure, function
and resources of a medical school, that the medical school
satisfies the standards established pursuant to section 4; and
“accreditation” shall be construed accordingly;
“award” means a degree, diploma, certificate or other evidence
that a medical programme of study has been completed
successfully;
“course” means a defined body of knowledge, skills and attitudes
designed to be inculcated over a specified period and to
which credits may be awarded;
“credit” means a unit of academic measurement for a prescribed
course or set of courses for which a particular qualification
is awarded;

Chap. 39:02

“Medical Board” means the medical board established by section
3 of the Medical Act;
“medical programme of study” means a curriculum comprising a
series of courses in medical education leading to an award
or credit at an institution of higher education;
“medical school” means an educational entity with a defined
structure providing for the administration, governance,
delivery and certification of a range of medical programmes
or courses leading to the granting of awards or credits;
“Minister” means the Minister responsible for Health;

3\. The Medical Board is responsible for conducting and
deciding on the accreditation of medical schools.

Authority of the Medical
Board to accredit.

4\. (1) The Medical Board shall establish standards and
procedures for the accreditation of medical schools.

Medical Board to
establish standards.

(2) The standards and procedures established by the
Medical Board under subsection (1) may relate to –
(a) the mission and objectives of a medical school;
(b) governance of a medical school;
(c) the administration of a medical school;
(d) the training and qualification of the faculty;
(e) the admission and recruitment of students;
(f) the educational programme, including education
of students in the basic sciences;
(g) clinical education;
(h) facilities and resources;
(i) certification processes and procedures;
(j) review of substantive changes;
(k) the procedure to be adopted on the closure of a
medical school.
5\. (1) The Medical Board shall –
(a) consider applications for accreditation submitted
to the Medical Board pursuant to section 7;
(b) determine whether to accredit and re-accredit
medical schools operating in Dominica;

Action to be taken by
Medical Board.

(c) maintain a registry of the medical schools
accredited;
(d) ensure that the quality of medical programmes of
study offered in Dominica meet the standards set
by the Medical Board for an award;
(e) ensure the maintenance of the appropriate
standards set by the Medical Board;
(f) assess the compliance of medical schools with
the standards and procedures established pursuant
to section 4;
(g) advise the Minister on the issuance of charters,
licences or other authorisations to medical schools
to operate or to continue their operations or to
alter their existing operations on the basis of an
appropriate evaluation;
(h) fix and collect fees in connection with the exercise
of its functions;
(i) revoke a certificate of accreditation granted to, or
conferred on a medical school if the Medical
Board considers that there is good and sufficient
cause to do so; and
(j) do or cause to be done such other things as the
Medical Board considers expedient or necessary
to achieve its objectives pursuant to this Act.
(2) The Medical Board is responsible for the proper
performance of its functions under this section but it may for the
purpose of such performance, as it considers appropriate, consult
with or obtain advice from other persons or authorities or engage
other persons to assist it in carrying out its functions.

## PART III

ACCREDITATION OF MEDICAL SCHOOLS
6\. (1) A person operating a medical school which is not
accredited shall not advertise or in any manner hold the medical
school out to the public as an accredited medical school.

Prohibition on falsely
representing without
accreditation.

(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits an
offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine of fifty
thousand dollars or imprisonment for two years, or both.
7\. (1) A person who wishes to have a medical school
accredited shall make an application to the Medical Board in the
manner specified in subsection (2).

Application for
accreditation.

(2) The application shall be –
(a) made to the Medical Board;
(b) in writing in the prescribed form; and
(c) accompanied by the prescribed fee.
(3) On receipt of an application, the Medical Board shall
consider whether the applicant complies with this Act and meets
the relevant requirements prescribed in Regulations or standards
made pursuant to this Act.
(4) The Medical Board is not, in considering an
application, restricted to the information contained in the
application.
8\. (1) The Medical Board may, by giving notice to the
applicant, require the applicant to provide to the Medical Board
within the time specified in the notice, such further information
which the Medical Board may reasonably require to make its
decision on the application.

Request for further
information to support
application.

(2) An applicant is taken to have withdrawn the application
if within the time stipulated in the notice the applicant does not
comply with the notice.
Additional matters to be
considered by the
Medical Board.

9\. In addition to the matters to be considered by the Medical
Board pursuant to section 7(3) the Medical Board shall in
considering an application for accreditation take into account the
following matters:
(a) the mission of the medical school;
(b) the goals and objectives of the medical school;
(c) the conditions under which the goals or objectives are
expected to be achieved;
(d) the expected standards and how those standards are
to be maintained;
(e) a description of the programmes of study or courses
to be offered and the policy regarding course credits;
(f) any links with other accredited medical schools;
(g) a student registry and admissions policy;
(h) the maintenance of student records;
(i) the availability of student counseling services and
procedures for monitoring the availability of such
services;
(j) in the case of recruitment of faculty and administration
into a medical school (i) the recruitment procedures;
(ii) the curriculum vitae of faculty and
administration;
(iii) the general productivity measures relating to
faculty performance;

(k) examination regulations and procedures with particular
emphasis on how –
(i) written exams are set, marked and how
student appeals are conducted;
(ii) oral exams such as research proposals are
conducted;
(ii) the grading system is used as a basis of
evaluating student performance;
(l) the system for selecting external examiners and how
the examiners function;
(m) the availability of library and research facilities with
particular emphasis on(i) the adequacy of such facilities;
(ii) their linkages with other libraries and other
medical schools and institutions within and
outside the Caribbean;
(n) the proposed physical location of the medical school
with particular emphasis on –
(i) projections regarding student enrolment and
staff size;
(ii) provisions for classrooms, laboratories and
offices;
(o) the overall financing and management of the medical
school with particular emphasis on –
(i) the charter of the medical school;
(ii) the governing body of the medical school; and
(ii) costs and efficiency of the medical school;

(p) the availability of facilities, locally or otherwise, for
the provision of clinical education.
Decision on application.

10\. (1) On an application made under section 7 the Medical
Board may accredit a medical school.
(2) The Medical Board may accredit a medical school
subject to conditions.
(3) Where the Medical Board is considering rejecting an
application it shall inform the applicant of its reasons for considering
refusing the application and provide the applicant with an opportunity
to respond prior to making a decision on the application.
(4) The Medical Board shall, for the record, prepare a
report outlining its consideration of each application and reasons
for the decision taken by the Medical Board on that application.
(5) The Medical Board shall, within a reasonable time of
arriving at a decision, communicate its decision in writing to the
applicant.
(6) The Medical Board shall ensure that the name of any
medical school whose application for accreditation has been
approved is placed in a Registry of Accredited Medical Schools
to be instituted and maintained by the Medical Board, and shall
cause that information to be published in the Gazette.
(7) The decision of the Medical Board with regard to an
application or any matter under this section or any other provisions
under this Part shall be determined –

Chap. 39.02

(a) during a meeting at which a quorum of the
members of the Medical Board are present, in
accordance with the Medical Act; and
(b) by unanimous consent of the members present at
the meeting;

11\. (1) Where the Medical Board approves an application
made pursuant to section 7 the Medical Board shall, within a
reasonable time, issue to the applicant a certificate of accreditation
as may be prescribed.

Issuance of certificate of
accreditation.

(2) The certificate of accreditation shall state the period
of validity of accreditation and it remains the property of the
Medical Board and the person in whose favour the certificate is
issued shall return it to the Medical Board upon its withdrawal,
revocation or expiry.
12\. A certificate of accreditation is valid for a period not
exceeding five years.

Validity of certificate of
accreditation.

13\. The application process for reaccreditation is the same as
that specified in section 7.

Reaccreditation.

14\. The Medical Board and its staff shall, subject to the laws
of Dominica, keep confidential all information obtained by it or its
staff in the assessment of a medical school provided that nothing
in this Act shall be deemed to prevent the Medical Board from
disclosing to a governmental body information reasonably sought
by that body in the performance of its lawful duties.

Confidentiality.

15\. (1) An accredited medical school shall offer to all clients
a standard of service that is consistent with the terms and
conditions specified in this section or which may be prescribed and
the conditions subject to which it has been accredited.

Conditions of
accreditation.

(2) It is a condition of accreditation that the Medical
Board is given reasonable access to the premises of the medical
school and the cooperation necessary to enable the Medical
Board or its representatives to monitor compliance with the terms
and conditions of accreditation, this Act and Regulations made
pursuant to this Act.
(3) Pursuant to subsection (2), the accredited medical
school shall make available to the Medical Board or its
representatives all information and relevant documents.

(4) An accredited medical school shall (a) at all times comply with the terms and conditions
of this section and with the standards for
accreditation prescribed by the Medical Board;
(b) only claim that it is accredited in respect of those
activities which are approved by the Medical
Board and where the medical school wishes to
operate outside the scope of its accreditation, the
medical school shall give sufficient notice to the
effect that the activity is not one that has been
accredited by the Medical Board;
(c) pay promptly such fees for application, assessment
and surveillance and such other services as shall
from time to time be determined by the Medical
Board with the approval of the Minister;
(d) not use the certificate of accreditation in such a
manner as to bring the arrangements for
accreditation into disrepute; and
(e) upon expiry or revocation of the certificate of
accreditation, forthwith discontinue its use or
reference to accreditation and withdraw all
advertising matter which contains any reference
to accreditation.
(5) An accredited medical school that wishes to refer to
the fact that it has been accredited by the Medical Board in its
documents, brochures or advertising media shall use the following
phrase:
“listed in the Dominica Registry of Accredited
Medical Schools.”.

16\. The Medical Board or any person who has been authorised
by the Medical Board may carry out unscheduled surveillance
visits to a medical school that has been accredited at intervals
other than those which may be prescribed.

Board may carry out
assessments and
surveillance visits.

17\. (1) A person who is aggrieved by a decision of the
Medical Board has a right to have the decision reviewed on
making an application to the Minister within 30 days after being
given notice of the decision.

Appeals.

(2) Where an application is made in accordance with
subsection (1), the Minister shall cause an inquiry to be conducted
concerning the matters raised in the application by any person or
persons as the Minister appoints in writing for that purpose.
(3) An inquiry shall be conducted according to the
substantial merits of the case without regard to technicalities.
(4) A person conducting an inquiry pursuant to subsection
(2) is not bound by any rules of evidence and may conduct the
inquiry and obtain information as he considers appropriate.
(5) On completing the inquiry, the person who conducted
the inquiry shall report to the Minister stating his findings and
recommendations and the Minister shall be bound by any findings
and recommendations made by such person.
(6) The Minister shall submit in writing to the applicant
who has lodged the appeal, the findings of the inquiry.
(7) The decision of the inquiry shall be final and subject
only to judicial review.

## PART IV

ENFORCEMENT
Enforcement action may
be taken.

18\. The Medical Board shall take enforcement action against
an accredited medical school where the Medical Board has
reasonable grounds to believe that a holder of a certificate of
accreditation has breached any of the conditions made pursuant
to this Act or issued by the Medical Board, or any Regulations
made hereunder.

Possible enforcement
action.

19\. (1) The Medical Board may take any of the following
enforcement actions it considers appropriate in light of the nature
of the violation committed by the holder of a certificate of
accreditation (a) warnings;
(b) revocation of certificate of accreditation;
(c) recommendation to the Minister that action be
taken against the holder of the certificate of
accreditation for a contravention of section 23(1).
(2) The Medical Board shall move to revoke a certificate
of accreditation only if the accredited medical school fails to take
corrective action within a reasonable time for violations identified
in writing by the Medical Board.

Notice of enforcement
action.

20\. (1) If the Medical Board believes that grounds exist for
enforcement action, the Medical Board shall issue to the holder of
the certificate of accreditation a notice that contains the following
information (a) the action which the Medical Board proposes to
take;
(b) the grounds for the proposed action;
(c) an outline of the facts and circumstances leading
to the decision; and

(d) an invitation to the holder of the certificate of
accreditation to show within a specified period
reasons why the proposed action shall not be
taken.
(2) The specified period shall be a period ending at least
thirty days after notice pursuant to subsection (1) is given to the
holder of the certificate of accreditation.
21\. (1) The holder of a certificate of accreditation may make
written representations with respect to the notice and the Medical
Board shall consider all such representations.

Holder of certificate may
make representation.

(2) If, after considering the representations made pursuant
to subsection (1), the Medical Board no longer believes that
grounds exist for enforcement action, the Medical Board shall not
take further action with respect to the notice and as soon as
practicable shall give notice to the accredited medical school that
no further action will be taken.
22\. (1) If after considering the representations made pursuant
to section 21(1), the Medical Board believes that grounds exist to
revoke a certificate of accreditation, the Medical Board shall, as
soon as practicable inform the holder of the certificate of
accreditation of its decision.
(2) The Medical Board shall ensure that the name of any
medical school whose certificate of accreditation has been
revoked is removed from the Registry of Accredited Medical
schools and shall cause that information to be published in the
Gazette and at least one newspaper in general circulation in
Dominica.

Revocation of certificate
of accreditation.

## PART V

MISCELLANEOUS
Offences.

23\. (1) The holder of a certificate of accreditation shall not–
(a) violate a condition of its accreditation imposed
pursuant to this Act, any Regulations made
hereunder or by the Medical Board;
(b) refuse to comply with a request for information
made by or on behalf of the Medical Board;
(c) deny access to relevant records, books or facilities
to any person authorised by the Medical Board to
obtain the information or obstruct that person in
the performance of his duties; or
(d) give information that he knows is false or has no
reason to believe to be true.
(2) A person who contravenes subsection (1) commits
an offence and is liable on summary conviction to a fine of fifty
thousand dollars or to a term of imprisonment not exceeding one
year, or both.

Medical Board may
revoke or deny
accreditation.

24\. The conviction of a person under section 23 does not
preclude the Medical Board from also revoking a certificate of
accreditation or from denying a certificate of accreditation to an
applicant.

Recommendation of
permanent closure.

25\. The Medical Board may recommend to the Minister the
permanent closure of a medical school in relation to which there
has been a contravention of section 23(1).

Regulations.

26\. The Minister may make Regulations for giving effect to
the provisions of this Act.
