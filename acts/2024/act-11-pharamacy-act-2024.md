## PART II

PHARMACISTS
Division 1- Registration of pharmacists
Registration.

### 22.

A person qualifies for registration as a pharmacist where

he –
(a) holds a degree, certificate, diploma or other form of
qualification granted by a university or school that in
the opinion of the Council is evidence of satisfactory
training; and
(b) satisfies the Council that he –
(i) is medically fit to practice pharmacy;
(ii) is a fit and proper person to be registered; and
(iii) can read, write, speak and understand the
English language; and
(c) there is no other issue which the Council considers
would affect his ability to practise pharmacy in
Dominica competently and safely.
Application for
registration.

### 23.

(1) A person who wishes to be registered as a pharmacist
in Dominica shall (a) apply to the Council in the prescribed form;
(b) pay the prescribed application for registration
fee; and
(c) meet the requirements under this Act.
(2) An application under subsection (1) must be
accompanied by –
(a) evidence of the qualifications of the applicant;
(b) proof of identity of the applicant;
(c) proof that the applicant is a fit and proper person

to practise pharmacy in Dominica;
(d) any other information that the Council may require.
(3) Where the Council requires further information from
an applicant in order to make a decision respecting registration, the
Council shall promptly, in writing, request the information required
to make the decision.
(4) The Council shall make a determination respecting the
applicant’s qualification for registration and notify the applicant, in
writing, of that determination –
(a) within sixty days of the submission to the Council
by the applicant of the requested information; or
(b) immediately after the expiration of sixty days
from the date when the request was made, in the
case where the information requested by the
Council has not been received by the Council.

### 24.

(1) Where an application is made to the Council under
section 23 and the Council is satisfied that the applicant meets the
relevant requirements specified in section 22 the Council shall, on
payment by the applicant of the prescribed registration fee,
register the applicant and subject to subsection (2), issue a
certificate of registration in the prescribed form in respect of the
applicant.
(2) A certificate of registration shall specify(a) the name and address of the holder of the certificate
of registration;
(b) the registration number of the registered person;
(c) the conditions, if any, subject to which the person

Refusal of registration.

(3) A certificate of registration is not transferable.

### 25.

(1) The Council may refuse to register an applicant on the
following grounds:
(a) the applicant does not qualify for registration
under section 22;
(b) the character of the applicant is such that it would
not be in the public interest to allow the applicant
to practise pharmacy;
(c) the applicant has a physical or mental condition
which significantly impairs his ability to practise
pharmacy;
(d) the applicant had previously been registered as a
pharmacist in another jurisdiction and his
registration was cancelled on grounds that would
justify similar cancellation in Dominica.
(2) Where the Council, having considered an application
made under section 23 and any further information received, and
having regard to subsection (1), is of the opinion that the application
should be refused, the Council shall, subject to subsection (3),
notify the applicant of its decision in writing.
(3) In a notice under subsection (2), the Council shall give
reasons for the intention to refuse the application and shall inform
the applicant of his right to make written representations to the
Council under subsection (4).
(4) An applicant who receives a notice under subsection

(2) may make written representations to the Council within
fourteen days after receipt of the notice under subsection (1).
(5) The Council shall have regard to any representations
made by the applicant within the time specified under subsection
(4) and shall, within fourteen days of the receipt of the written
representations, give notice in writing to the applicant of its
decision, the reasons for refusal, if any, and the applicant’s right
to appeal under section 70.

### 26.

The Council shall cancel the registration of a pharmacist
where it is satisfied that the pharmacist does not qualify for
registration or is disqualified from registration by virtue of the
circumstances referred to in section 25(1)(b) to (d).

### 43.

DOMINICA PHARMACY COUNCIL
1\. (1) The Council shall consist of seven members appointed
by the Minister as follows:
(a) the Chairperson who shall be a person who has been
registered as a pharmacist in Dominica for a continuous
period of not less than ten years;
(b) two pharmacists who have been registered in Dominica
for not less than five years, on the nomination of the
Association of Pharmacists;
(c) the Chief Pharmacist, who shall be an ex officio
member;
(d) a registered medical practitioner on the nomination of
the Medical Association;
(e) an attorney-at-law nominated by the Dominica Bar
Association who has been admitted to practice law in
Dominica for not less than seven years;
(f) one member of the public who does not represent any
particular interest or body.
(2) he member appointed under subparagraph (1)(c) is a
non-voting member.
(3) The members of the Council shall appoint as the
Deputy Chairperson one of the pharmacists referred to in

subparagraph (1)(b).
2\. (1) Subject to subparagraph (2), members of the Council,
other than the Chief Pharmacist shall hold office as follows:
(a) the Chairperson, for a term of three years;
(b) other members, for a term of two years.
(2) For the initial period of appointment to the Council, the
pharmacists referred to in paragraph 1(1)(b) shall be appointed
for three years.
(3) Members of the Council are eligible for re-appointment
and, except in respect of the Chief Pharmacist, shall hold office
for not more than two consecutive terms, but
a member is
eligible for re-appointment after the expiration of one year
following the end of the two terms.
(4) The Chairperson of the Council shall continue to hold
office until a new Chairperson is appointed or until he is reappointed Chairperson, as the case may be.
3\. A person is disqualified from being a member of the
Council and shall not become or continue to be appointed as a
member of the Council if the person is or has been –
(a) adjudged bankrupt under the Bankruptcy Act;
(b) declared by a court to be physically or mentally
incapacitated by reason of unsoundness of mind;
(c) convicted of a criminal offence except where the
offence is a minor traffic offence or has been spent.
4\. (1) The Chairperson or the Deputy Chairperson may
resign his respective office by letter addressed to the Minister and

the resignation takes effect from a date notified in the letter, or
immediately, where no date has been so notified.
(2) A member may at any time resign his office by letter
in writing addressed to the Chairperson, and on the date of receipt
of the letter, that person ceases to be a member of the Council.
5\. The Minister may, at any time, in writing, revoke the
appointment of a member of the Council if, upon evidence, the
Minister is satisfied that the member is –
(a) disqualified from being a member of the Council
under paragraph 3;
(b) guilty of neglect of duty, misconduct or malfeasance;
or
(c) has failed to act in the best interests of the Council.
6\. A person ceases to be a member of the Council if he –
(a) is absent from Dominica without leave for a period of
six months or more;
(b) fails without reasonable excuse to attend two
consecutive meetings of the Council; or
(c) for whatever reason fails to attend meetings for a
period of twelve months.
7\. (1) Whenever there is a vacancy in the membership of the
Council, it shall be filled in the same manner in which the original
appointment was made.
(2) A person appointed to fill a casual vacancy shall
hold office only so long as the member in whose stead he is

appointed would have held office.
8\. The validity of any proceedings of the Council shall not be
affected by any vacancy among the members of the Council or
by any defect in the appointment of a member.
9\. (1) The Chairperson, or in his absence the Deputy
Chairperson, shall preside at all meetings of the Council.
(2) The Council shall –
(a) meet regularly, at least once in every three months;
and
(b) at any other times that may be necessary or expedient
for the transaction of the business of the Council.
(3) The Council shall hold meetings at places and times
and on days that the Council may determine.
(4) At least seven days notice in writing must be given to
members of the Council of any meeting of the Council.
10\. The Chairperson shall summon a special meeting of the

of the Council.
11\. Three members of the Council constitute a quorum, one
of whom must be the Chairperson.
12\. (1) Subject to paragraph 1(2), every –
(a) member of the Council is entitled to vote;

PROCEEDINGS OF THE COMPLAINTS
COMMITTEE
1\. (1) On receiving a complaint in accordance with section
63 or on initiating a complaint of its own motion the Council shall
–
(a) record the date on which the complaint was
received or initiated; and
(b) within thirty days of receipt or initiation of the
complaint, determine whether to accept or reject
the complaint.
(2) The Council may require the complainant to provide
further particulars of a complaint.
2\. Whenever a matter is referred by the Council to the
Complaints Committee, the Chairperson of the Committee shall
promptly notify, in writing, the pharmacist whose conduct or
action is the subject of the investigation.
3\. The notice referred to in paragraph 1 shall –
(a) identify the matter that is to be investigated;
(b) shall stipulate the right of the pharmacist to submit
to the Complaints Committee in writing, any
explanation or representation he wishes to make
as well as any document or other information,
concerning the matter that he wishes to submit to
the Committee;

(c) shall indicate that any submission must be made
by a date not later than twenty-eight days from
the date of the notice or such other time as
stipulated by the Complaints Committee.
4\. The Complaints Committee shall examine all documents
and other information relating to the complaint that are submitted
to it and shall make every reasonable effort to verify the information
and ascertain all the facts that are relevant to a determination of
the matter.
5\. The Complaints Committee may hear evidence from any
person, whether or not the person is a party concerned in the
matter.
6\. Following a consideration of the information submitted to
it, the Committee, where it determines that it is in the best interest
of all the parties concerned, may solicit clarifications, in writing,
with respect to any statement, representation or other information
submitted to the Committee.
7\. In the case where the Complaints Committee is conducting
an investigation other than an investigation under section 64(7)(a)
(i) or (ii) (a) the Committee may invite each of the parties or
both parties concerned in the matter to appear
before the Committee to make representations or
to answer questions respecting the matter;
(b) the Committee may, on the request of a party,
permit the party to appear before the Committee
and is not required to give notice to the other party
regarding the appearance but may allow the other
party to have a copy of any statement or

representation made to the Committee or an
extract of such statement or representation;
(c) the pharmacist, where he appears before the
Committee, has the right to be represented by an
attorney-at-law.
8\. The Complaints Committee shall report its findings,
decision and recommendation to the Council in accordance
with section 9(8) of the Act.

PROCEEDINGS OF THE DISCIPLINARY
COMMITTEE
1\. (1) The Council shall serve on the pharmacist against
whom disciplinary proceedings are to be instituted a notice of
hearing which shall –
(a) specify in the form of a charge, the matters into
which the hearing is to be held; and
(b) state the time and place at which the Disciplinary
Committee intends to hear the matter.
(2) Except with the consent of the pharmacist, a hearing
shall not be fixed for a date earlier than twenty-eight days after
the date of the notice of hearing.
(3) The notice of a hearing shall be served personally or
by registered post on the pharmacist at the address shown on the
register or at his last known address if that address differs from
that on the register.
(4) Except with the consent of the pharmacist, a hearing

shall not be fixed for a date earlier than twenty-eight days after
the date of the notice of hearing.
(5) The notice of hearing shall be served personally or by
registered post on the pharmacist at the address shown on the
register or at his last known address if that address differs from
that on the register.
2\. The pharmacist is entitled to receive free copies of, or to
be allowed access to, any documentary evidence relied on for the
purpose of the hearing.
3\. The pharmacist has the right to be represented by an
attorney-at-law in any disciplinary proceedings instituted against
him.
4\. Where the pharmacist does not appear at the date fixed
for the hearing of the case, the Disciplinary Committee may, if it
is satisfied that a notice of hearing has been served on the
pharmacist, proceed with the hearing in his absence and the
absence of his attorney may proceed with the hearing, and without
further notice to the pharmacist, take any action that it is
authorised to take under this Act.
5\. (1) Where witnesses are examined by the Disciplinary
Committee , the pharmacist shall be given an opportunity of
attending and of putting questions to the witnesses in his own
behalf; and no documentary evidence shall be used against him
unless he has previously been supplied with copies of those
documents or given access to the documents.
(2) The pharmacist shall be permitted to give evidence,
call witnesses and make submissions orally or in writing in his own
behalf.
(3) The Disciplinary Committee may call additional

witnesses and may adjourn the proceedings to another convenient
time or place.
(4) The testimony of witnesses shall be taken under oath,
which the Chairperson of theDisciplinary Committeeis authorised
to administer, and there shall be a full right to examine, crossexamine and re-examine witnesses.
(5) The Disciplinary Committee may issue summons for
the purposes of procuring the attendance of witnesses, the giving
of testimony and the production of documents before the
Disciplinary Committee.
(6) The rules of evidence in a hearing, and the proceedings
and penalties in the case of disobedience to any summon, shall be
the same as in civil cases in the Supreme Court.
6\. The Disciplinary Committee shall have a record of the
proceedings and the evidence –
(a) taken in shorthand, transcribed and certified by a
professional transcriber; or
(b) recorded by a sound recording machine, and transcribed
and certified by a professional transcriber.
7\. Where, having heard the evidence in support of the
charges, the Disciplinary Committee is of the opinion that the
evidence is insufficient, it may dismiss the complaint without
calling on the pharmacist for his defence.
8\. On the conclusion of the hearing, the Disciplinary

Committee shall submit a report to the Council in accordance with
section 66.
