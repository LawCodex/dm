### 1.

This Act may be cited as theADMINISTRATION OF ESTAlES BY
CONSULAR OFFICERS ACT.

### 2.

Whenever any subject or citizen of any country mentioned in Administrationof
estates by
the first column of the Schedule consularofficers.
(a) dies within the State; or

Sdtedule.

(b) dies outside the State, leaving property within the State,

and no person is present in the State at the time of his death who is
rightfully entitled to administer the estate of the deceased person, the
consul, vice-consul, or consular agent of the country within the State
may take possession and have the custody of the property of the
deceased person, and may apply the same in payment of his debts and
funeral expenses, and may retain the surplus for the benefit of the
persons entitled thereto; but the consul, vice-consul, or consular agent
shall immediately apply for, and shall be entitled to obtain from the
Court, letters of administration of the property of the deceased person,
limited in such manner and for such time as to the Court seems fit.

### 3.

The President may by Order vary the Schedule (a) by deleting therefrom any country when the provision

of the treaty with that country mentioned in the Schedule has ceased to have effect;
(b) by adding thereto any country with whom the State

shall make a treaty of commerce and navigation containing provision similar to any of the provisions
mentioned in the Schedule.
