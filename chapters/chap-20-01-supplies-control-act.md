## CHAPTER 20:01

SUPPLIES CONTROL ACT
AN ACT to provide for the maintenance and regulation of supplies and for
other matters incidental thereto and connected therewith.
Commencement

[17 January 1980]
Short title

---

### 1.

This Act may be cited as the—
SUPPLIES CONTROL ACT.
Interpretation

### 2.

In this Act—
“Controller” means a public officer appointed as Controller of Supplies by the
Minister;
“consumer” means any person who purchases or offers to purchase goods otherwise
than for purpose of resale;
“Consumer Protection Officer” means an officer appointed as such for the
enforcement of prices in respect of price controlled goods;
“export” means to take or to be concerned in taking out of Dominica or the territorial
waters thereof any goods or commodities, and “exportation” shall be
construed accordingly;
“goods” include all kinds of movable or personal property including animals, plants
and birds;
“hoard” means laying or putting any article aside for the purpose of accumulating or
overstocking it;
“import” means to bring or to be concerned in bringing into Dominica or the
territorial waters thereof, any goods or commodities, and “importation” shall
be construed accordingly;
“landed cost”, in relation to imported goods, means the total of the following:
(a) either—
(i)

the c.i.f. value of goods less all discounts on the article; or

(ii)

the c.i.f. value of goods determined by the Controller of
Customs and Excise;

(b) customs duties and charges (excluding warehouse rent);
(c) cost of remittance to the place of payment;
(d) an additional sum of two and one half per cent of the total amounts
referred to at (a), (b) and (c);
(e) in case of cold storage, an amount equal to the commercial cold store
rate in force at the time, or where there is no such rate, such
reasonable sum as the Controller of Supplies may think fit;
“maximum price” means the maximum price of goods fixed by the Minister in
accordance with the provisions of section 6;
“Minister” means the Minister responsible for supplies control and regulation of
prices;
“price” includes any valuable consideration of any kind whatsoever whether direct
or indirect;
“price-controlled goods” means goods the selling price of which is controlled by an
Order under section 4;
“retail” refers to the sale of goods in any quantity specified in the definition of
“wholesale”;
“retailer”, in relation to any description of goods, means a person who in the
ordinary course of his business sells goods of that description by retail;
“sell”, with its grammatical variations and cognate expressions, includes an
agreement to sell and offer to sell, the exposure of goods for sale, the
publication of a price list, the furnishing of quotation, or any act or
notification whatsoever whereby willingness to enter into any transaction of
sale is expressed;
“trader” means any person who is engaged in the sale or offer for sale of an article to
which this Act applies; and includes any partnership, firm or incorporated
company engaged in such sale or offer for sale;
“wholesale” refers to the sale of goods in any quantity not less than that contained in
the smallest package in which the goods are ordinarily sold by wholesalers to
retailers; or where the goods are not ordinarily sold in packages by
wholesalers to retailers, the smallest quantity of the goods which is ordinarily
sold by wholesalers to retailers; but notwithstanding the foregoing definition
of “wholesale”, any sale of goods to a person who deals in the goods for
resale shall be deemed to be a sale by wholesale;
“wholesaler”, in relation to any description of goods, means a person who in the
ordinary course of business sells goods of that description by wholesale.
(Amended by Act 12 of 1990)

Appointment and functions of Controller

### 3.

(1) The Minister may appoint a suitably qualified public officer to be the
Controller for the purposes of this Act.
(2) Without prejudice to the succeeding provisions of this Act, it shall be the
function of the Controller to recommend programmes for maintaining, controlling
and regulating supplies so as to—
(a) secure a sufficiency of those commodities or goods essential to the
well-being of the community, the equitable distribution thereof and
their availability at fair prices;
(b) ensure that exports and imports of commodities or goods are
calculated to serve the interest of the community; and
(c) ensure generally that the commodities or goods available to the
community are used in a manner calculated to serve the interests of
the community.
(Amended by Act 12 of 1990)
Minister may by Order control importation or exportation of goods

### 4.

(1) The Minister may by Order—
(a) prohibit absolutely the importation or exportation of goods of any
class or description from or into any country;
(b) prohibit the importation or exportation of goods of any class or
description except under the authority of a licence issued by the
Controller;
(c) control the prices at which goods of any class or description may be
sold whether by wholesale or retail.
(2) An Order made under this section may provide for such supplementary
and incidental matters as may be necessary or expedient for the purpose of the
Order.
Grant of import licences

### 5.

(1) Where an Order made by the Minister under section 4 prohibits the
importation or exportation of any goods except under licence issued by the
Controller, the Controller may, subject to this section, grant or withhold licences for
the importation or exportation of the goods.
(2) A licence granted under this section—
(a) may be either general or limited to a specific person;
(b) may be absolute or conditional;

(c) may be limited so as to expire on a specified date unless renewed;
and
(d) may be revoked by the Controller if he considers it is in the public
interest to do so;
but if a licence is revoked as aforesaid any person aggrieved thereby may appeal
from the decision of the Controller to a tribunal consisting of three public officers of
Permanent Secretary rank appointed for the purpose by the President acting in his
own deliberate judgment, and the tribunal shall decide whether the revocation is in
the public interest, and may either confirm or reverse the revocation.
(3) The Minister may give the Controller directions of a general character as
to the policy to be followed by him in the performance of his functions under this
section and the Controller shall act in accordance with such directions.
(4) Any person who contravenes any term or condition or restriction subject
to which any licence is granted by the Controller under this section is liable on
summary conviction to a fine of three thousand dollars and to imprisonment for
three months.
Maximum price of goods

### 6.

An Order made by the Minister under section 4 may direct that the maximum
price of any goods shall not exceed a specific sum or may direct the manner in
which the maximum price may be ascertained, and may—
(a) fix a maximum price at which any class of goods shall be sold for a
prescribed period;
(b) fix different prices for the same class of goods having regard to the
terms and condition upon which and the circumstances in which the
goods are manufactured, imported or sold and the place in Dominica
in which the goods are sold to customers; and
(c) fix alternative methods by which the maximum price may be
ascertained for the same or different classes of goods.
Sale of goods at proportionately increased or decreased prices

### 7.

Where the quantity of goods sold or offered for sale is greater or less than the
unit quantity specified in an Order made under section 4(1)(c), the goods shall,
subject to any express provisions to the contrary in the Order, be sold or offered for
sale at a proportionately increased or reduced price, as the case may be.
Sale of goods greater than the maximum price prohibited

### 8.

(1) Subject to the provisions of section 7, no person shall, in respect of any
goods the maximum price of which has been fixed by an Order under section
4(1)(c)—

(a) sell or agree to sell any such goods at a greater price than the
maximum price;
(b) impose, whether before or at the time of delivery of any such goods
or at any subsequent time prior to payment therefor, any condition
relating to the sale of such goods, including any condition for the
purchase of any other goods, or for the payment of any premium or
other like sum or for the giving of any consideration in respect of
such goods other than the immediate payment of the permitted
purchase price; or
(c) with the object of speculating or profiteering, hoard such goods.
(2) Any person who contravenes any of the provisions of subsection (1) of
this section is liable—
(a) on summary conviction to a fine of seven hundred and fifty dollars
and to imprisonment for three months; or
(b) on conviction for a second or subsequent offence to a fine of four
thousand dollars and to imprisonment for two years.
(3) Any person who knowingly purchases any commodity above the
controlled price is guilty of an offence under this Act.
Fixing of any average price

### 9.

(1) Notwithstanding the provisions of any Order made under section 4 but
subject to the provisions of section 8(1), the holder of a stock of identical goods
which may have been received on different consignments and at varying prices may
with the consent of the Controller fix an average price for the goods and the price
approved by the Controller shall be the maximum wholesale or retail price, as the
case may be, for the goods.
(2) Where price-controlled goods are sold in a package provided by the
seller, the Controller shall allow an addition to the maximum selling price of such
amount as he may think just to cover the cost of packing as well as of the package.
Storing of price-controlled articles restricted

### 10.

(1) From and after the coming into effect of this Act, no wholesaler or
retailer shall, for the purposes of his trade or business, store any price-controlled
article on any premises or in any place other than his trade or business premises
without the consent in writing of the Controller.
(2) No person shall permit any price-controlled article to be stored on any
premises or in any place in his possession or under his control in contravention of
subsection (1).
(3) The occupier in control of any premises or place in which pricecontrolled articles are found in contravention of subsection (1) shall be deemed, in

the absence of a reasonable explanation, to have contravened that subsection and is
guilty of an offence against this Act.
(4) For the purposes of this section business premises include any storehouse
in which the wholesaler or retailer has been in the habit of keeping his stock in trade
or which the wholesaler or retailer has acquired for the said purpose.
Refusal to sell

### 11.

(1) No wholesaler shall, without reasonable cause, the proof whereof lies on
him, refuse to sell to any retailer, if immediate payment of the maximum permitted
price is tendered, the normal quantity of any articles usually sold to such retailer.
(2) No retailer shall, without reasonable cause, the proof whereof lies on
him, refuse to sell by retail to any person if immediate payment is tendered, a
reasonable quantity of any article demanded and which is required for use or
consumption by an intending purchaser.
(3) Without prejudice to the generality of subsection (1), a vendor shall be
deemed to refuse without reasonable cause to sell any goods if he falsely denies that
he has the goods or uses any words or gives any other indication calculated to lead a
person who wishes to buy the goods to believe erroneously that the vendor has no
such goods.
(4) Without prejudice to the generality of subsection (1), a vendor shall be
deemed to have reasonable cause to refuse to sell any goods if the sale would,
having regard to the quantity required or any other consideration, be contrary to the
normal practice of his business or would involve or lead to a breach of some
obligation lawfully binding on him or would interfere with arrangements made by
him for an orderly disposal of his stocks among his customers.
(5) Any person who contravenes this section is liable on summary conviction
to a fine of seven hundred and fifty dollars and to imprisonment for three months.
Delivery of invoice

### 12.

(1) Any trader who sells whether for cash or for credit any goods shall, if so
required by the purchaser, forthwith furnish the purchaser with a bill in which he
shall set out a full description of the goods, the quantity sold, the date of the
transaction and the price paid by the purchaser.
(2) Any person who contravenes subsection (1) is guilty of an offence.
(3) Subsection (1) does not apply to vendors in public markets.
Record of cost to be kept

### 13.

(1) Every trader shall keep a record of all particulars relating to the cost of
goods imported or otherwise purchased by him so as to enable the Consumer
Protection Officer or any person authorised by the Controller to readily trace the
landed cost or the cost of acquiring such goods.

(2) Any person who contravenes subsection (1) is guilty of an offence.
(Amended by Act 12 of 1990)
Verification of prices

### 14.

(1) The Consumer Protection Officer or any person authorised by the
Controller is hereby empowered—
(a) to require any trader from time to time to supply him either orally or
in writing with any information in his possession relating to any
goods offered for sale;
(b) at any reasonable time to require any trader to produce for his
inspection any document or account whatsoever relating wholly or in
part to the business carried on by the trader in relation to any goods
offered for sale;
(c) to require any trader to furnish him with copies of any such
documents as referred to in paragraph (b) for the purpose of
verifying the prices of the goods;
(d) to enter during business hours upon any business place or premises
in the occupation of any trader for the purpose of ascertaining
whether the provisions of this Act are being complied with and to
search for and inspect any documents referred to in paragraph (b)
and make copies or abstracts and take such steps as may be
reasonably necessary for the aforesaid purpose;
(e) to take possession of any document referred to in paragraph (b)
which appears to him to constitute prima facie evidence of a breach
of any of the provisions of this Act or of any Order made thereunder.
(2) Any person who—
(a) assaults, obstructs, impedes, hinders, molests or refuses admission to
a Consumer Protection Officer or to an authorised person in the
exercise of his powers under subsection (1); or
(b) wilfully fails to give any information or to produce any document
lawfully required of him under subsection (1); or
(c) knowingly gives any false information orally or in writing relating to
goods,
is guilty of an offence.
(Amended by Act 12 of 1990)
Position of price list

### 15.

Every vendor of any article sold by wholesale or retail shall keep posted in
public view in some conspicuous place on his business premises a list in good and

readable condition showing the prices of goods offered for sale by him, or shall
clearly mark on the goods or on the wrappings or containers of the goods, so as to be
clearly seen and read by intended purchasers, the prices at which the goods are
offered for sale.
Powers of search and inspection

### 16.

(1) If in respect of any premises, the Controller has reasonable grounds for
suspecting that an offence has been or is being committed against this Act or any
Order or Regulations made thereunder and that evidence of the commission of the
offence is to be found on the premises, and is satisfied—
(a) that it is essential in the public interest that the premises should be
searched for the purpose of obtaining the evidence; and
(b) that the evidence is not likely to be found at the premises unless they
are forthwith searched,
the Controller may, by written order under his hand, authorise any person named in
the order or a police officer to enter the premises by force if necessary and search,
seize and take away any article found therein which the persons carrying out the
search have reasonable grounds for believing to be evidence of the commission of
the offence.
(2) The Controller or Consumer Protection Officer, or any person authorised
in writing by the Controller, or a police officer, may, at any time during the business
hours of any shop in which any goods are offered for sale, enter and inspect the
goods and any price list or mark indicating the price relating thereto.
(3) Any person who assaults, obstructs, impedes, hinders, molests or refuses
admission to the Controller or any officer authorised in writing by the Controller, the
Consumer Protection Officer or a police officer is guilty of an offence.
(Amended by Act 12 of 1990)
Sale by order of Controller

### 17.

(1) Whenever a trader possesses stocks of any goods of which there is
otherwise a shortage on the local market, the Controller may without requisitioning
the goods, by order in writing, require the trader to sell to such person or persons as
shall be named in the order the quantities therein specified of the goods, and upon
tender of immediate payment of the price of the quantities stipulated in the order.
(2) Any trader who without reasonable excuse, the proof whereof lies on
him, fails to comply promptly with the order of the Controller, upon tender of the
purchase price by a purchaser named in the order, is guilty of an offence.
Body corporate charged with offence

### 18.

Where a person charged with an offence under this Act is a body corporate
every person who, at the time of the commission of the offence, was a director or

officer of the body corporate may be charged jointly in the same proceedings with
the body corporate and where the body corporate is convicted of the offence every
such director so charged shall be deemed to be guilty of that offence unless he
proves that the offence was committed without his knowledge or consent or that he
exercised due diligence to prevent the commission of the offence.
General liability

### 19.

Every person shall be liable for the acts and omissions of his partners, agents
and servants in so far as they relate to the business of such person, and if any
partner, agent or servant performs any act or suffers any omission which contravenes
this Act such person and his partner, agent or servant, as the case may be, may be
charged jointly and severally under this Act, and may be convicted unless he proves
that the offence was committed without his knowledge or consent or that he
exercised due diligence to prevent the commission of the offence.
Compensation to purchaser where goods sold above maximum price

### 20.

Where a person is convicted of selling goods at a price in excess of the
maximum price and—
(a) the Court considers that the purchaser of the goods did not know and
could not reasonably be expected to know that the price paid by him
was in excess of the maximum price; or
(b) the purchaser of the goods reported the sale to the Controller or a
police officer within forty-eight hours of the sale,
the Court may, in addition to any other penalty which it may impose, order the
person convicted to pay to the purchaser the difference between the maximum price
of the goods and the price at which the goods were sold together with such sum, if
any, as the Court may think fit to award by way of compensation for any expenses
incurred by the purchaser.
Intimidation

### 21.

(1) No person shall in any manner intimidate or victimise or attempt to
intimidate any witness who has given or is about to give evidence against such
person or any other person in any proceeding under this Act, or who has been or is
in any way concerned in the prosecution or intended prosecution of any person
under this Act.
(2) Any person who contravenes subsection (1) is liable on summary
conviction to a fine of seven hundred and fifty dollars and to imprisonment for three
months.
Disclosure of information

### 22.

(1) No person who obtains information by virtue of this Act or any Order
made thereunder shall disclose that information otherwise than in the discharge of

his functions under this Act or under any such Order, or for the purpose of any
criminal proceedings.
(2) Any person who contravenes this section is liable on summary conviction
to a fine of seven hundred and fifty dollars and to imprisonment for three months.
Advisory Committee

### 23.

(1) The Minister may appoint an Advisory Committee to advise him on the
making of Orders under section 4 and may make Regulations prescribing the powers
and duties of the Committee and of the members thereof.
(2) Regulations made under this section may provide for the remuneration of
members of the Advisory Committee in the performance of their duties.
Importation of prohibited goods

### 24.

Nothing in this Act shall make it lawful to import or export goods when the
importation or exportation is unlawful by any other written law.
Orders to continue to have effect

### 25.

Any Orders made under the Supplies (Control) Act in force immediately
before the commencement of this Act shall continue to have effect until revoked by
or under the authority of Regulations made under this Act.
Fraction of a cent

### 26.

Whenever the maximum selling price of goods includes a fraction of a cent
then—
(a) if the fraction is less than half a cent it shall be ignored;
(b) if the fraction is half a cent or more, it shall count as a cent.
No sale of price-controlled goods unless costing submitted

### 27.

(1) Except with the written permission of the Controller, an importer shall
not sell any price-controlled goods imported by him unless he has submitted
costings and other documentary evidence showing at what price the goods are to be
sold and he has received the checked and approved copy from the Controller.
(2) If within three days of submitting the costings and other documentary
evidence as mentioned in subsection (1), the importer does not receive either the
checked and approved copy, or notice of rejection of the proposed price giving
reasons for the rejection, he shall be deemed to have received the checked and
approved copy.
(3) An importer who sells any price-controlled goods imported by him
without the written permission of the Controller is guilty of an offence.
(Amended by Act 12 of 1990)

Licence may be suspended

### 28.

If a person is convicted on three occasions after the coming into force of this
Act for either—
(a) selling any price-controlled goods above the maximum permitted
price; or
(b) refusing to keep a price list posted in public view,
the Magistrate may order that his licence to carry on his business be suspended for
any period not exceeding six months from the date of the order.
Controller may take possession of goods

### 29.

It shall be lawful for the Controller, the Consumer Protection Officer, a
customs officer, any police officer or any person appointed by the Controller for the
purposes of this Act, to take possession of any articles or goods the importation and
exportation whereof is subject to licensing, which he has cause to suspect were
imported or are intended for export by any person other than under and in
accordance with the provision of a licence granted by the Controller and to charge
such person before a Court for contravening the provisions of this section. On
conviction of such person the goods shall be forfeited and handed over to the
Controller for disposal by sale at public auction.
(Amended by Act 12 of 1990)
Protection of officers

### 30.

No liability, civil or criminal, shall attach to any person in respect of any act
done in the bona fide exercise of his duties or functions under this Act.
Penalty

### 31.

Any person guilty of an offence against this Act for which no penalty is
expressly provided is liable on summary conviction to a fine of one thousand five
hundred dollars and to imprisonment for six months.
Regulations

### 32.

(1) The Minister may from time to time make Regulations—
(a) prescribing the forms of licences and of any other forms to be used;
(b) for prescribing anything that needs to be prescribed under this Act;
and
(c) generally for the carrying out of the purposes of this Act.

(2) Regulations made under this Act may provide penalties not exceeding
seven hundred and fifty dollars on summary conviction for contravention of any
provision in a regulation.
